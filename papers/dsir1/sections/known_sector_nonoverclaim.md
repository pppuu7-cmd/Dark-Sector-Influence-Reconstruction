## Known-sector specificity control: matter geometry is not a dark-sector identity statistic

A retrospective post-unblinding control provides an important limit on how the response geometry should be interpreted. In Exp071D, an ordinary known-sector baryon-fraction path (`K2_baryon_fraction`) is itself highly concentrated in one matter-response principal direction, with a first-PC fraction of

\[
0.9990439690,
\]

while its endpoint-progress coordinate is non-monotonic and its maximum adjacent-tangent turn reaches

\[
169.692^\circ.
\]

Thus low apparent dimensionality, near-directionality, or simple matter-response geometry cannot by themselves be treated as dark-sector-specific signatures or as evidence for a unique microscopic parameter map. This is consistent with the DSIR operator view: matter-space geometry is a mechanism/transfer diagnostic whose specificity depends on the retained channels and analysis operator. Independent Weyl, slip/lensing, temporal, or other physically distinct information is required for stronger identification claims.

This control is **retrospective and post-unblinding**. Exp071D explicitly introduces no new hard gate or threshold and is not promoted to a dark-sector specificity theorem. We use it only to constrain interpretation of the preceding response-atlas results; it does not alter any prospectively frozen DSIR-I result and does not close G7, G8, or G9.
