# Data, code, and reproducibility

DSIR-I is designed so that the scientific record does not depend on conversational context or on a final plotting notebook. The analysis code, frozen experiment summaries, validation contracts, scientific PASS/FAIL classifications, and manuscript claim ledger are maintained in the public Dark-Sector Influence Reconstruction repository. Each central quantitative manuscript statement is linked by the Paper-I provenance matrix or a manuscript-scoped evidence extension to a workflow run, immutable GitHub Actions artifact or commit, SHA256 digest where available, frozen criterion, and interpretation boundary.

The paper distinguishes four forms of reproducibility evidence. First, **operator controls** verify identities internal to the analysis, including reconstruction closure, orthogonality, normalization, signed-spectrum semantics, and repeatability. Second, **numerical-provider controls** verify solver limits, source state, unit conventions, interpolation semantics, and state integrity. Third, **scientific prospective controls** freeze a prediction or acceptance rule before evaluating a new amplitude, family, mechanism, or operator realization whenever the result is intended to support a directional claim rather than provide descriptive morphology. Fourth, **public-input reproduction controls** bind exact released survey objects by checksum and reproduce masks, redshift distributions, catalog-to-pixel mappings, or other operator inputs before any physical-support or downstream likelihood quantity is read.

Scientific failures are immutable provenance. A failed experiment is not reclassified when a later experiment identifies a justified correction. For example, the original C3 GDM target-grid physical-power bridge remains failed at a roughly 4.75% reconstruction defect; a later mechanism audit localized the defect to the frozen interpolation construction, and a separately preregistered native-grid provider passed at a \(2.81\times10^{-14}\) matter-power closure. Likewise, the original designer-\(f(R)\) q=1 bridge remains failed against the frozen \(5\times10^{-6}\) GR-limit criterion even though a prospective numerical-accuracy ladder and separately frozen q=3 provider subsequently pass the same physical boundary without floor subtraction or threshold relaxation.

The same rule applies to physical generalization and observational-operator construction. A common C3/C5 full-response-centroid relation was frozen before evaluation on a withheld IDM--DR mechanism and failed with all four slopes of the opposite sign to the calibration. That failure remains part of the paper evidence. Likewise, a finite DES harmonic operator class was identified prospectively, but the independently frozen exact-realization audit failed mandatory reproduction/provenance before any support fraction was computed. DSIR therefore did not relabel the candidate as usable; the failed parent is retained while a public DES Y1 real-data replacement was selected prospectively under unchanged future support criteria.

Undefined theory/channel cells are never zero-imputed. Cross-family statistics are evaluated only on explicitly defined support, and high-\(k\) WDM products remain a distinct block from the frozen low-\(k\) C1/C2/C3/C5 atlas. Theory-space response angles, \(\chi_I\), and \(\eta_I\) are not reported as signal-to-noise ratios or survey-level significances. The final observational signature construction additionally requires a finite positive support measure, an exact reproducible real-data operator/input realization, a support-eligible physical/window operator, validated covariance whitening, and a prospectively frozen nuisance quotient. A support fraction is itself treated as undefined when the prospectively chosen positive operator measure is nonnormalizable; no retrospective ultraviolet cutoff or fiducial-power weighting is introduced merely to force a finite normalization.

The late DES harmonic-route work illustrates this hierarchy. The public replacement preserves the future 5% physical-support threshold and minimum retained dimension. Subsequent frozen stages bind the released inputs by SHA256, reproduce the public mask and redshift distributions, and then reproduce raw-catalogue sky coordinates as the expected HEALPix pixels. The latest completed prerequisite sampled 131,072 rows from the 136,930,995-row parent catalogue across 16 frozen windows and recovered the required source/metacalibration fields and pixel indices in all four source bins at \(N_{\rm side}=4096\). Its artifact explicitly records that the physical science gate was not scored; this is an operator-reproduction prerequisite, not a physical-support PASS, covariance result, nuisance-rank result, or advance of G7/G8/G9. The detailed M--R chronology is retained in the supplement and observation-route ledger.

Publication figures are generated deterministically from repository products by manuscript-scoped plotting scripts. The figure manifest records exact input products, scientific selection rules, and caption boundaries. Plotting scripts may change presentation details, but they may not silently change the scientific domain, mask, normalization, response orientation, or frozen threshold. Final figure outputs are accompanied by checksums in the build artifact.

The manuscript is maintained on a dedicated public paper branch and reviewed through draft pull request #99. Exact branch names, workflow identifiers, artifact IDs and recovery paths are recorded in the repository provenance files rather than repeated as long machine strings in the journal prose. Chat-independent recovery checkpoints remain part of the repository; recovery should proceed from those files rather than reconstructed conversational memory.
