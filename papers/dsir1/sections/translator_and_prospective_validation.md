## Cross-model translation robustness and mixed prospective validation

The historical Paper-I plan included a cross-model translator and independent withheld-family tests. The final repository pass closes both requirements, but at a deliberately weaker level than a universal model map or a survey-level equivalence relation.

For a retrospective C3--C5 translator audit, define the finite response coordinate

\[
q=\bigl[\ln k_{\rm geo},\ z_{\rm centroid},\ \ln\chi_I\bigr]
\]

on the frozen GDM-viscosity and designer-\(f(R)\) amplitude rays. Nearest-neighbour translation was recomputed under three common coordinate scalings (pooled z-score, min--max, median/MAD) crossed with three norms (\(L_1,L_2,L_\infty\)). In all **9/9** full-coordinate variants, the finite-grid cycle

\[
{\rm C3}\rightarrow{\rm C5}\rightarrow{\rm C3}
\]

returns to the same C3 grid point for exactly **2/5** sampled amplitudes. The large-amplitude endpoint, \(c_v^2=10^{-4}\rightarrow B_0=10^{-3}\), is stable in all nine variants, whereas the low- and intermediate-amplitude points collapse many-to-one onto a shared C5 representative in eight of nine variants. This is therefore a robust finite-grid statement of **non-bijective translation**, not a microscopic parameter identity.

Coordinate ablations make the response-subspace dependence explicit. Keeping \((\ln k_{\rm geo},z_{\rm centroid})\) preserves the same 2/5 cycle closure in all 9 variants. Removing scale localization and retaining only \((z_{\rm centroid},\ln\chi_I)\) reduces the cycle closure to **1/5** in all 9 variants and maps all five C3 points to \(B_0=10^{-3}\). The translator is thus local, multicoordinate, and conditional on the retained response subspace. Because this audit uses already-frozen summaries, it is a retrospective robustness test rather than a prospective observational validation.

The prospective record provides an independent and deliberately mixed PASS/FAIL check of the response-language methodology. First, the preregistered C8 interacting-dark-matter--photon test evaluated an endpoint-normalized half-transition relation on a mechanism whose response had not been used to select its coupling grid. All 35 model--redshift rows possessed a unique half-transition crossing, but the four frozen adjacent slopes were

\[
\mathcal C_{50}=\{-7.8081,\ +4.9485,\ -3.0590,\ +5.4661\}.
\]

The first and third adjacent pairs violate the preregistered strict-positive sign condition, and the same failed-pair pattern survives **all seven** leave-one-redshift deletions. This is a hard prospective FAIL. The relation is not rescued by changing the normalization, grid, crossing definition, sign convention, or acceptance rule after seeing C8.

Second, a genuinely withheld C9 interacting-dark-matter--baryon family was evaluated with a two-coordinate localization-plus-shape operator frozen from C3/C5/C7/C8 before the first C9 response. The four standardized adjacent path-step norms were

\[
\{0.438675,\ 2.91023,\ 5.76186,\ 0.0477483\},
\]

all above the preregistered \(10^{-10}\) floor; the C9 polyline has no nonadjacent intersections, and **all seven** leave-one-redshift operator rebuilds independently pass the same frozen gate. This is a hard prospective PASS for the specified multicoordinate path criterion.

The negative C8 result and positive C9 result are scientifically complementary. They show that DSIR's prospective machinery can both reject an attractive scalar generalization and retain a more flexible multicoordinate representation on a fresh mechanism without retrospective repair. They do **not** establish a universal translator, a common dark-sector law, an observational likelihood improvement, or a post-G7 G8 validation. G7, G8, and G9 remain open.
