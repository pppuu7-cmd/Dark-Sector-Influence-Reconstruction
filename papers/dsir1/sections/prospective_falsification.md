## Prospective falsification of a common response-centroid law

A response atlas is scientifically useful only if it can reject an appealing common description when a prospectively withheld mechanism disagrees. We therefore retain a negative cross-family test as part of the central DSIR-I evidence rather than treating failed universalizations as discarded development history.

Using only immutable C3 generalized-dark-matter and C5 designer-\(f(R)\) products, a common full-response localization operator was defined by

\[
q_k^R(k)=\frac{\sum_z R(z,k)^2}{\sum_{z,k}R(z,k)^2},
\qquad
k_R^{\rm geo}=\exp\!\left(\sum_k q_k^R\ln k\right),
\]

and an adjacent source-response slope

\[
{\cal C}_i=\frac{\Delta\ln k_R^{\rm geo}}{\Delta\ln k_*}.
\]

Before any response from the withheld C7 interacting-dark-matter--dark-radiation family was generated, the C3/C5 calibration fixed the acceptance interval

\[
0.0022992621\le {\cal C}_i\le0.0995121922.
\]

The complete C7 science contract was committed before the first C7 matter-power response was generated. In the first science-evaluable run, the source-native scales were

\[
k_* = \{0.08485,0.07348,0.06000,0.04647,0.03928\}\;h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1},
\]

whereas the measured full-response centroids were

\[
k_R^{\rm geo}=\{0.06628,0.08089,0.09263,0.09796,0.09915\}\;h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}.
\]

All four adjacent slopes had the opposite sign to the calibration,

\[
{\cal C}=\{-1.3856,-0.6685,-0.2191,-0.07157\},
\]

and therefore failed the frozen interval. The scientific classification is

`FAIL_IDM_DR_COMMON_SOURCE_RESPONSE_SLOPE_V0_1`.

This failure is not repaired by widening the interval, reversing the expected sign, or redefining the withheld response coordinate. Post-gate diagnostics show why the candidate operator failed: as the IDM--DR coupling increases, the raw \(R^2\) localization becomes dominated by the upper boundary of the finite \(k\)-window, while the same C7 responses are almost perfectly scale-time separable on the frozen domain. A full-response power centroid that tracks a transition for nonseparable C3/C5 responses therefore need not track the source-native scale for an almost scale-only mechanism.

The result constrains, rather than undermines, the main DSIR-I conclusion. Observable influence geometry is mechanism and operator dependent, and a single scalar coordinate should not be universalized without a fresh prospective test. The broader use of response localization as a descriptive organizing language remains viable, but this specific C3/C5/C7 common law is falsified and G7/G8 remain open.
