## 8.1 Closest neighboring approaches

Several neighboring approaches clarify what DSIR-I does and does not add. The general program of mapping dark-sector microphysics to perturbative observable signatures predates DSIR: Bashinsky explicitly framed a mapping from cosmological observables to dark kinetics [@Bashinsky2007DarkKinetics], while the dark-energy ``fingerprinting'' series mapped equation-of-state, sound-speed, clustering and viscosity/anisotropic-stress information into matter, lensing and related observables [@SaponeKunz2009Fingerprinting; @SaponeKunzAmendola2010Fingerprinting; @SaponeMajerotto2012Fingerprinting]. Recent work continues this program using effective sound-speed information to compare dynamical dark energy and modified-gravity perturbations [@ReboucasEtAl2026Sound]. DSIR-I therefore does **not** claim invention of dark-sector fingerprinting, of sound-speed/viscosity-to-observable mapping, or of the qualitative principle that an independent metric or velocity-sensitive channel can break a density-sector degeneracy.

Principal-component and model-agnostic modified-gravity analyses likewise already identify combinations of response functions that a survey can constrain efficiently [@HojjatiEtAl2012; @ZanolettiLeonard2025], while observable-space model-breaking measures quantify how strongly a survey can depart from a restricted model manifold [@AmaraRefregier2014]. Modern dark-degeneracy analyses also show concretely that models which are degenerate in the background can separate once perturbative observables are included [@PetriMarraVonMarttens2026]. DSIR does not claim invention of PCA, phenomenological response functions, observable-space compression, or perturbation-based degeneracy breaking. Its more specific contribution is to compare heterogeneous pre-existing mechanism families in one block-aware response geometry, quantify irreducible scale-time structure and finite-amplitude trajectory curvature, define exact equivalence conditionally on the retained operator, preserve prospective cross-mechanism failures, and retain the physical/numerical eligibility chain that must be passed before a survey-level distance is assigned.

The nonlinear boundary also has close precedents. Generalized-dark-matter studies have developed nonlinear phenomenology, halo-model treatments, spherical-collapse extensions, and current Euclid-like forecasts with dedicated nonlinear GDM matter modelling [@KoppSkordisThomas2016; @ThomasKoppMarkovic2019; @PaceSakrTutusaus2020; @SakrLopezSanchez2026]. These results motivate nonlinear GDM modelling, but they do not make the frozen DSIR C3 pressure/viscosity vector a unique nonlinear theory: choosing a particular completion can introduce additional physical assumptions, which is why DSIR separates model-definition uncertainty from provider accuracy. Likewise, the WDM and DCDM families used here are established physical benchmarks rather than new microscopic models [@BodeOstrikerTurok2001; @VielEtAl2005; @PoulinSerpicoLesgourgues2016]. Their role in DSIR-I is to stress the response representation with scale-localized and time-localized mechanisms, not to replace dedicated WDM or decaying-dark-matter likelihood analyses.
