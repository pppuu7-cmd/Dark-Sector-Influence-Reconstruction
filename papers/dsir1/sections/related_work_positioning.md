## 8.1 Closest neighboring approaches

Several neighboring approaches clarify what DSIR-I does and does not add. Principal-component and model-agnostic modified-gravity analyses already identify combinations of response functions that a survey can constrain efficiently [@HojjatiEtAl2012; @ZanolettiLeonard2025], while observable-space model-breaking measures quantify how strongly a survey can depart from a restricted model manifold [@AmaraRefregier2014]. Modern dark-degeneracy analyses also show concretely that models which are degenerate in the background can separate once perturbative observables are included [@PetriMarraVonMarttens2026]. DSIR does not claim invention of PCA, phenomenological response functions, or observable-space compression. Its distinct use of these ideas is to compare several heterogeneous mechanism families in one block-aware response atlas, define exact equivalence conditionally on the retained operator, and preserve the physical and numerical eligibility tests that must be passed before a survey-level distance is assigned.

The nonlinear boundary also has close precedents. Generalized-dark-matter studies have developed nonlinear phenomenology, halo-model treatments, and spherical-collapse extensions [@KoppSkordisThomas2016; @ThomasKoppMarkovic2019; @PaceSakrTutusaus2020]. These results motivate nonlinear GDM modelling, but they do not make the frozen DSIR C3 pressure/viscosity vector a unique nonlinear theory: choosing a particular completion can introduce additional physical assumptions, which is why DSIR separates model-definition uncertainty from provider accuracy. Likewise, the WDM and DCDM families used here are established physical benchmarks rather than new microscopic models [@BodeOstrikerTurok2001; @VielEtAl2005; @PoulinSerpicoLesgourgues2016]. Their role in DSIR-I is to stress the response representation with scale-localized and time-localized mechanisms, not to replace dedicated WDM or decaying-dark-matter likelihood analyses.
