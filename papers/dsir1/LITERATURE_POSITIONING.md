# DSIR-I literature positioning and publication assessment

**Date:** 2026-08-27

## Executive conclusion

A targeted literature search found several closely related research lines, but no direct precedent for the full DSIR-I combination of:

1. a multi-family response atlas spanning DE, IDE, GDM, WDM, DCDM and modified gravity;
2. an explicit irreducible `k x z` response-morphology component and finite-amplitude/node-robustness tests;
3. channel-conditional equivalence formalized through `A_B = Q_B W_B K_B`;
4. explicit separation of microscopic parameter count, manifold dimension, representation rank and discriminant count;
5. prospective/withheld cross-mechanism tests including a genuine prospective falsification;
6. preservation of scientific FAILs as immutable provenance rather than retrospective correction;
7. a fail-closed **observation-route eligibility hierarchy** separating finite positive normalization, exact real-data reproducibility, physical support, model/provider semantics, covariance whitening and nuisance quotienting.

This is not proof of universal novelty. It is the current targeted positioning and must be repeated immediately before submission.

## Closest prior lines

### Dark degeneracy

**Martin Kunz, “The dark degeneracy: On the number and nature of dark components,” Phys. Rev. D 80, 123001 (2009), arXiv:astro-ph/0702615.**

Core overlap: gravity constrains total observable influence rather than a unique named decomposition of the dark sector.

DSIR-I difference: it operationalizes a heterogeneous response atlas and asks which explicit channels, scale-time structures and observational operators split or preserve equivalence classes. Kunz is foundational and must be cited as such.

### Modern pairwise dark-degeneracy breaking

**V. Petri, V. Marra, R. von Marttens, “Dark Degeneracy in DESI DR2: Interacting or Evolving Dark Energy?”, Phys. Rev. D 113, 023504 (2026), arXiv:2508.17955.**

Core overlap: identical or nearly identical background behavior need not imply identical perturbative observables.

DSIR-I difference: the DESI analysis is a concrete pairwise IDE/dynamical-DE inference, whereas DSIR attempts a general multi-family response/operator language and explicitly tracks the channel-dependent kernel of the comparison.

### Model-independent dark-sector reconstruction

**R. von Marttens et al., “Model-independent reconstruction of dark sector interactions,” Phys. Rev. D 104, 043515 (2021), arXiv:2011.10846.**

**L. A. Escamilla et al., “Model-independent reconstruction of the interacting dark energy kernel: Binned and Gaussian process,” JCAP 11 (2023) 051, arXiv:2305.16290.**

Core overlap: phenomenological inference without fixing one microscopic interaction law.

DSIR-I difference: these reconstruct within an interacting-dark-sector class; DSIR compares several qualitatively different mechanisms in one response geometry.

### Model-agnostic modified-gravity PCA

**A. Hojjati et al., “Cosmological tests of General Relativity: a principal component analysis,” Phys. Rev. D 85, 043508 (2012), arXiv:1111.3960.**

**C. M. A. Zanoletti and C. D. Leonard, “Principal Components for Model-Agnostic Modified Gravity with 3x2pt,” Phys. Rev. D 112, 063547 (2025), arXiv:2503.20951.**

Core overlap: identify informative response directions and degeneracies with low-dimensional data/response representations.

DSIR-I difference: PCA/compression is not claimed as new. DSIR focuses on cross-family response manifolds, irreducible scale-time morphology, channel-conditioned equivalence and the distinction between a mathematically available projection and a physically admissible realized operator.

### Observable-space / model-breaking geometry

**A. Amara and A. Refregier, “Model Breaking Measure for Cosmological Surveys,” Phys. Rev. D 89, 083501 (2014), arXiv:1309.5955.**

Core overlap: distinguish model space from observable space and characterize the constraining geometry of an experiment.

DSIR-I difference: DSIR is not primarily a survey figure of merit. It asks how heterogeneous physical mechanisms trace response trajectories and, before constructing a covariance-weighted distance, tests whether the chosen realized observational route is itself scientifically admissible.

### PPF / EFT / generalized stress-tensor frameworks

Relevant foundations include Hu & Sawicki PPF, Baker--Ferreira--Skordis PPF, EFT-of-dark-energy literature, and Hu’s generalized dark matter.

Core overlap: common phenomenological descriptions bridge heterogeneous underlying models and separate background from perturbations.

DSIR-I difference: these are theory/phenomenology bases. DSIR uses such families as inputs to a comparison protocol. Exp073D/E sharpen an important boundary: a phenomenological linear closure need not uniquely define a nonlinear continuation, so selecting a nonlinear provider can add model-defining physics rather than merely numerical accuracy.

### Nonlinear GDM and weak-lensing / harmonic-operator methods

The bibliography includes nonlinear-GDM work, KiDS/DES multi-probe studies, pseudo-`C_ell`/NaMaster-style harmonic analysis, and small-scale mitigation/nulling approaches.

Core overlap: nonlinear model semantics, survey projection and small-scale sensitivity are precisely the issues encountered when a theory-response comparison is pushed into real observation space.

DSIR-I difference: it refuses to use these tools as an implicit licence for a hidden completion. The Exp073B--R0 chain separates questions that are often blended together:

- can the projector accept the required physical blocks?;
- is nonlinear provider physics available and model-defined?;
- is the positive support measure finite?;
- is the exact public real-data realization reproducible?;
- does that realized operator actually live on the certified physical domain?;
- only then: what is the covariance/nuisance-quotiented distance?

The Exp073N/O pair is particularly useful for publication positioning. A finite and physically plausible DES harmonic **operator class** is not accepted merely because the associated paper/data release exists: the frozen exact real-data Y3 realization failed mandatory provenance. DSIR then selected a public Cosmotheka DES Y1 replacement prospectively without changing the future physical-support threshold. This appears to be a useful methodological distinction between *published/operator-class availability* and *reproducible realized operator eligibility*.

### Recent theory-space classification around LambdaCDM

**M. Naeem, “LambdaCDM as a fixed point: Controlled dark-sector deformations and late-time structure growth,” Annals of Physics 490 (2026) 170466.**

Core overlap: organize departures from LambdaCDM as a structured theory/response space rather than one isolated model.

DSIR-I difference: DSIR compares multiple existing mechanism families and studies their response geometry/equivalence under different channels, rather than constructing a controlled deformation basis around one fixed point.

## Where DSIR-I appears genuinely differentiated

The strongest publication-level novelty is **not** “model-independent dark sector,” “PCA,” or “response space” in isolation. The most defensible differentiators are:

- **Cross-mechanism atlas** under one comparison protocol.
- **Explicit quotient equivalence:** `A_B=Q_BW_BK_B`, with equivalence induced by the selected physical/observational channel.
- **Scale-time nonseparability:** the additive `mu+T(k)+tau(z)` core is falsified on the tested atlas and `chi_I` yields a robust descriptive hierarchy.
- **Mechanism-localized degeneracy breaking:** matter lookalikes split in slip; scale-mode lookalikes split in temporal/full response.
- **Curved response manifolds:** one microscopic parameter does not imply one linear representation mode.
- **Prospective failure as evidence:** the withheld IDM-DR common-centroid law fails and remains failed.
- **Provider/reproduction FAIL preservation:** later corrected providers or replacement operators do not rewrite earlier failed contracts.
- **Observation-route admissibility before statistics:** finite normalization, exact real-data reproducibility, physical support and model/provider semantics are separate prerequisites.
- **Operator-class vs realized-operator distinction:** Exp073M is a candidate-class FOUND, Exp073N is an exact-realization provenance FAIL, Exp073O is a prospectively selected replacement FOUND, and Exp073P2/S0/R0 are increasingly deep prerequisite PASSes. None is silently promoted to the physical-support gate.

The article should foreground this combination rather than claim novelty for any one standard ingredient.

A second risk is terminological overreach. “PASS operator,” “support PASS,” “survey equivalence,” and “detectability” must never be used interchangeably. The paper’s current distinction among candidate, exact-realization provenance, prerequisite reproduction, physical support, covariance and nuisance quotient is a strength if maintained consistently.

## Journal fit

### JCAP — strongest current fit

The paper combines cosmological perturbation theory, phenomenological model comparison, survey-operator methodology and reproducible computation. This remains the most natural first target if the final article stays framework + response atlas + admissibility methodology.

### Physical Review D — plausible, higher novelty bar

PRD becomes more attractive if the operator/equivalence theorem and the realized-operator admissibility result are presented as a sharp general methodological contribution rather than an implementation chronicle.

### MNRAS — strong alternative

MNRAS is a credible route if the article leans more strongly into cosmological observables, public survey operators and reproducible numerical methodology.

1. Do not wait for G8/G9 or a universal residual law.
2. Do not require a successful covariance quotient as a condition for DSIR-I publication.
3. Continue the already frozen DES/BOSS route only in order: exact reproduction -> physical support -> covariance -> nuisance quotient. A FAIL at any gate is itself a legitimate result and blocks later stages.
4. Keep late route details in the supplement unless they materially change the main conclusion.
5. Target JCAP first under the current scope; consider PRD if the formal/operator-admissibility contribution becomes the dominant narrative; retain MNRAS as a strong alternative.
