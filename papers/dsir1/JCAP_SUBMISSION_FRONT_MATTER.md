## JCAP-ready abstract

We introduce Dark-Sector Influence Reconstruction (DSIR), a model-agnostic framework for comparing heterogeneous dark-sector and modified-gravity mechanisms through their observable response geometry rather than through microscopic model labels alone. We construct a common response atlas spanning smooth dark energy, interacting dark sectors, generalized dark matter, thermal warm dark matter, decaying dark matter, and designer modified gravity. On the frozen low-wavenumber atlas, a purely additive scale-plus-time representation is insufficient: the irreducible scale-time component produces a robust descriptive hierarchy from interacting dark-sector directions through smooth dark energy and generalized dark matter to designer modified gravity, with the class ordering preserved in all twelve deterministic single-node deletion tests. We show explicitly that degeneracy is channel conditional: directions that are nearly collinear in matter response can be strongly separated by metric slip, while scale-mode lookalikes separate through temporal evolution. Finite-amplitude response trajectories also show that microscopic parameter count, manifold dimension, and linear representation rank need not coincide. Finally, we make the admissibility of an observational quotient explicit. A formal survey projection is not used unless its positive support measure is finite, its exact real-data realization is reproducible, and its physical support is certified before covariance whitening and nuisance projection. Prospective audits expose failures at each of these layers and preserve them as scientific provenance rather than repairing them retrospectively. DSIR-I therefore establishes a response-classification and identifiability framework, not a universal dark-sector law or a claim of new fundamental physics.
